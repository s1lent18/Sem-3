
Factors contributing to mental disorders:

    -Biological:
        neurochemicals, neurotransmitters, hormones, brain, nervous system, etc

    - Psychology:
        cognitions, emotions, personality, coping styles, resilience, values, etc

    - Social:
        Social support, family relations, friends, interpersonal factors, love, affliliation

    DSM: Diagnostic Statistical Manual of mental disorders
        
        - it is a book for disorders publised by american psychiatric association

        - DSM only classifies disorders and describe the symptoms but does not explain cures

    Two Major Classifications of Mental Disorders:

        - Neurotic Disorders:

            distressing but one can still function in society and act rationally
            
            Diagnostics:
                generalized anxiety disorders
                depression
                OCD [Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder]
                Social Phobia
                PostTraumatic Stress Disorder
                Panic Disorder
                Anti-Social Disorder
        
        - Psychotic Disorders:

            person loses contact with reality, experiences distorted perceptions
            
            Diagnostics:
                Schizophrenia
                Brief psychotic Disorder
                Delusional Disorder
                Substance-induced Psychotic Disorder

        Mental disorders are usually associated with significant distress or disability
        in social, occupational or other important activities

        Major Depressive Disorder:

            - also known as depression
            - involves a depressed mood or loss of pleasure for a pro-longed period
            - typically people feel low irrespective of the time of the day
            - diagonosed after continous loss of interest in most of the activities after
              atleast 2 weeks

              Common Signs:
                Sleep Disturbance
                Suicidial Thoughts
                guilty feelings
                significant weight gain/loss
            
        Panic Disorder:

            - sudden episodes of panic or fear
            - can last for a few minutes or few hours
            - characterized as sense of losing control even when there is no triggering element

                Common Signs:
                    Chest Pain
                    hyperventilation
                    sweating
                    dizziness

            - people with the panic disorder may worry about when is the next panic attack
              gonna happen
            - panic attacks do not have any identifiable stimuli
            - frequent and severe panic attacks leads to the diagnosis of panic disorder

        Schizophrenia:

            - a mental illness that affects how a person thinks, feel, and behaves
            - people with Schizophrenia may feel a lost touch with reality
            - they can experience hallucinations and delusions
            - their mental abilities may dedoriate which forces the person to withdraw
              from social interactions
            
        OCD:

            - causes recurring, unwanted thoughts or sensations
            - People experience compulsions, irresistable urges to repeatedly carry out some act
            - resisting the urges causes great distress related to dire consequences
            - People with OCD may know that the thoughts are not realistic
            - Diagnostics are made on:
                
                * presence of obsesional thoughts
                * compulsions that are time consuming
                * causes significant distress
                * impair work or social funtioning

            - The OCD cycle:

                Obsessive thoughts -> anxiety -> Compulsive behavior -> temporary relief

        PTSD:

            - develops in people facing a shocking, scary, or dangerous event
            - outcomes:
                * hypervigiliance
                * dreams or flashbacks of the traumatic event
                * sleep Disturbance
                * poor concentration

            - diagnosis:
                - requires exposure to a traumatic event
                    * directly experiencing it
                    * witnessing a traumatic event happening to others
                    * getting to know about a traumatic event which involves a close relative
                    * 
            
            - Previously Was Know as:
                "Shell Shock" - WW1
                "combat fatigue: - WW2

        Bipolar Disorders:

            - disorders which causes high fluctuation in one's mood and energy
            - usually experience:
                * over excitement and activity
                * delusions
                * euphoria - mania
                * depression

            - These mood swings can affect sleep, energy, activity, judgment
              behavior and the ability to think clearly.

            - these mood episodes typically occur over distinct periods of days to weeks
            - one may feel over excited or Depressive

        Internet Gaming Disorder:

            - addiction of online gaming is refered to as internet gaming disorder
            - relates to caffiene addiction
            - causes significant impairment or distress in one's life
            - Symptoms:
                
                * sadness and anxiety when trying to withdraw
                * unsuccessfull attempts to quit gaming
                * risk, jeopardizing personal relations or work for gaming
                * loss of interest in other activities

            - Cases of IGD rapidly increased during covid
            - Gaming could be a symptom of an underlying problem, such as depression or anxiety,

        Caffeine Use Disorder:

            - Caffeine intoxication and caffeine withdrawal are included in DSM

        Treatment of Psychological Disorders:

            - Treatment depends on the type of mental illness its severity and what works best. 
            - usually a  a combination of treatments is prefered

            - Psychological approach:

                - helping the affecties and their familes through therapy sessions
                  including psychoanalysis, humanistic-oriented therapy,
                  cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT),

            - Biomedical approach:

                - use of medinices to treat mental disorders such as schizophrenia
                  depression, and anxiety, as well as the employment of
                  brain intervention techniques
                  including electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)
                  transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), and psychosurgery.

            - Social approach:

                - The social approach to reducing disorder focuses on changing the social
                  environment in which individuals live to reduce the underlying causes
                  of disorder. These approaches include group, couple, and family therapy, 
                  as well as community outreach programs. The community approach is likely to
                  be the most effective of the three approaches because it focuses
                  not only on treatment, but also on prevention of disorders [WHO] / 2004
