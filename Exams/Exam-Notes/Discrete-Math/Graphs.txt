Graph Terminology:
    -Simple Graphs:
        * undirected edges
        * multiple edges not allowed
        * self loops not allowed

    - Multi Graphs
        * undirected edges
        * multiple edges are allowed
        * self loops not allowed

    - Pseudograph Graphs
        * undirected edges
        * multiple edges are allowed
        * self loops are allowed

    - Simple Directed Graphs
        * directed edges
        * multiple edges not allowed
        * self loops not allowed

    - Directed Multi Graphs
        * directed edges
        * multiple edges are allowed
        * self loops are allowed

    - Mixed Graphs
        * undirected and directed edges
        * multiple edges are allowed
        * self loops are allowed

Definitions:
    adjacent and incident:
        Two vertices are "adjacent" of each other in a graph when there is an edge between them
        and the edge connecting the two vertices is called an "incident"
    
    Degree:
        The number of vertices connected to a specific vertex is called the degree of that vertex
        in the case of a self loop the degree is considered "2" i.e (1 in and 1 out)

Hand-Shaking Theorem:
    the sum of degrees of all the vertices must be equal to the twice number of edges
    i.e the total degree of a graph must be even

    an undirected graph has an even number of vertices with odd degrees

Directed Graphs:
    the in-degree of a vertex is denoted by deg- (name of the vertex)
    the out-degree of a vertex is denoted by deg+ (name of the vertex)

Special Simple Graphs:
    
    - Complete Graphs:
        * a simple graph that contains exactly one edge between each pair of distinct vertices

    - Wheels and Cycles

Bipartite Graphs:
    in simple words the adjacent nodes of a Bipartite graph must be representated with different
    colors

Adjacency Lists:
    an Adjacency list can be used to represent a graph with no multiple edges by specifying the
    vertices that are adjacent to each vertex of the graph

    an Adjacency list for an undirected graph:
        the column for vertices is named "vertices" and the second column will be named 
        "Adjacent vertices"

        the column for vertices is named "Initial Vertex" and the second column will be named
        "Teminal vertices"
    
Adjacency Matrices:
    each index represent the number of nodes connected to it
    in other words adjacency matrices will be transpose.

    The rows going from top to bottom represent vertices and the columns going left to right

Paths:
    a path is a sequence of edges that begins at a vertex of a graph and travels from vertex
    to vertex along edges of the graph. As the graph travels along its edges, it visits the
    vertices along this path, that is, the endpoints of these.

Euler Graphs:
    
    - Euler Paths:
        Euler path is a simple path containing every edge of the graph
    - Euler Circuit:
        * Euler Circuit is a simple circuit containing every edge of the graph
        * A connected multigraph with atleast two vertices has an euler circuit iff each of its 
          vertices has an even degree and it has an euler path iff it has exacly two vertices of
          odd degree

Hamilton Graphs:

    - Hamilton Paths:
        A simple graph in a graph that passes through every vertex exactly once is called a 
        Hamilton Path
    - Hamilton Circuit:
        A simple circuit in a graph that passes through every vertex exacly once is called a 
        Hamilton Circuit

        Dirac's Theorem:

            vertex is greater than equals to the number of vertices / 2.

        Ore's Theorem:
            if a simple graph have more than or equals to 3 vertices and the sum of degree of non
            adjacent vertices must be greater than or equals to the number of vertices

Planar Graphs:
    a graph is called planar if it can be drawn in the plane without any edges crossing.
    
    Graph to be planar:
        - total edges must be less than or equals to (number of vertices * 3) - 6.
        - total edges must be less than or equals to (number of vertices * 2) - 4.
